Think About It

What happens to the regression slope when you move an observation horizontally? When you move it vertically? Which direction creates more change?

Moving a point vertically creates a large residual and pulls the line toward it. Moving horizontally changes leverage: points far from the mean of X can strongly affect the slope even with a small residual. The combination of high leverage and large residual creates maximum influence.

Think About It

Can a high-leverage point have a small residual? Why might that still matter?

Yes. A point far from the mean of X but close to the regression line has a small residual but high leverage. It has the potential to affect the slope if it moves even slightly. Influence requires both leverage and a large residual.

Think About It

Should we remove an observation just because Cook's Distance is large? What else should we consider?

No. A large Cook's Distance indicates influence, not error. Investigate whether the data is correct, whether it represents a real phenomenon, and whether the model is misspecified. Deletion is a last resort.

Think About It

How could a wrong functional form create patterns in residual plots?

If the true relationship is nonlinear but you fit a straight line, the residuals will show a systematic curved pattern. The model tries to fit a curve with a line, leaving structured error that should be random.

Remedies Laboratory

Compare different statistical approaches on the same dataset. Remedies must match the underlying cause of the model problem.

Think About It

Why might robust regression be preferable to ordinary least squares? What are the trade-offs?

Robust regression downweights influential outliers so the fit reflects the majority of data. It sacrifices some efficiency on clean data but gains protection against contamination. The trade-off is that it no longer minimizes squared error exactly.
